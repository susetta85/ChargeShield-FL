\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report RQ1 first, then RQ3 and RQ2, and keep the natural regime and the
canary regime apart throughout: the canary tests the sensitivity of the
instrument, it does not answer a research question. All numbers are read from the result matrices
generated from the run records.

The per-record analysis tells a different story (Table~\ref{tab:perrecord}).
Of the 17{,}561 sessions that are members in at least two of the five seeds,
412 are flagged without DP, against $289.9$ expected by chance (standard
deviation $16.2$): $z = 7.56$, an excess of about 122 sessions ($2.35\%$ of
the sessions against $1.65\%$). These sessions are ranked systematically high
across independently trained models while the aggregate AUC of the same cell
is $0.51$. Since the composed score is, in this regime, a monotone function of
the loss, the result is a consistent ranking by reconstruction error, and we
report it as such rather than as the success of a calibrated attack.

\subsection{Client-Level DP at \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon \le 1$}{eps <= 1}}
\label{sec:results-dp-low}

At $\varepsilon \le 1$ per round, under every placement, the reconstruction
loss is between 108 and 179 times the no-DP reference
(Table~\ref{tab:campaign}), far beyond the acceptable cost of
Table~\ref{tab:spec}. In the same cells the per-record excess disappears
($z$ between $0.02$ and $0.62$, Table~\ref{tab:perrecord}). The two facts
admit two explanations that these data cannot separate: DP removed the signal,
or the model no longer learns the data and therefore has nothing to reveal.
These cells are not an operating point, and we do not read them as evidence
that DP protects.

Nor does the formal guarantee constrain the attacks there. Taken at face value
and composed over the 10 rounds, the per-round values give cumulative labels
of 10, 5 and 1; the corresponding bounds on the AUC of any membership test on
the client unit are $0.99996$, $0.993$ and $0.731$ (Section~\ref{sec:bg-dp}).
Only the smallest is not vacuous, and it concerns clients rather than the
records we attack. The pilot cells at $\varepsilon = 8$ and $16$ keep the cost
within $4.6$ and $2.6$ times the reference, but have one seed each and are not
a result.

\subsection{Instrument Validation}
\label{sec:results-canary}

The balanced canary control on Office~1 passes on the raw-loss surface
(Table~\ref{tab:canary}). Mean AUC is $0.745$ in the base arm and $0.715$ in
the exchanged arm; all 30 round-level values exceed $0.5$ (minimum $0.6125$);
all five per-seed sums exceed one; and all ten increases over the untrained
baseline are positive. The pattern is symmetric under role exchange, which is
the signature of membership rather than of template difficulty. Averaging the
two arms within each seed, the mean increase is $+0.230$ (standard deviation
$0.052$), $t = 9.90$ with four degrees of freedom ($p < 0.001$); the one-sided
sign test gives five of five ($p = 0.031$).

The shadow-calibrated LiRA score does not track the surface on which
memorisation is present: its mean canary AUC is $0.651$ and $0.696$ in the two
arms, and one of the 30 round-level values falls below $0.5$. The per-record
variance estimates hit their floor in 97--99\% of the records, so the
per-record calibration cancels from the ranking. A symmetry check of the
scoring rule, which should return $0.5$, returns $0.21$--$0.34$ under the
\texttt{central} placement. And an unplanned repetition of the whole campaign
with identical configurations and seeds reproduced the raw-loss AUC bit for bit
in all ten cells, while the LiRA AUC changed on one seed (from $0.616$ to
$0.649$ in arm A, from $0.632$ to $0.598$ in arm B). The target model is
deterministic under seeding; the shadow calibration is not. This is why the
raw loss is our primary metric and the LiRA scorer is frozen until the sweep is
complete.

The control establishes sensitivity in the canary regime on one site only.
Caltech and JPL have runs at a single seed without the balanced protocol, so
the control is \pending{to be repeated on Caltech}. With record-level DP at
noise multiplier 5, in the canary regime, the raw-loss AUC of the canaries falls
from $0.68$ to $0.53$ while the model remains trainable; this comes from one
seed without the exchanged arm, and we do not report its $\varepsilon$ until
the accountant is corrected.
